    How the score works

    Every paper on the day's arXiv math.CO listing (new submissions and cross-lists) is read from its abstract and given a score from 1 to 100: an estimate of the level of journal the result belongs in, weighing the strength of the result against prior work, whether it settles a known problem, and how wide an audience will care — with extra weight on structural, extremal and probabilistic graph theory. Only the 30 highest-scoring papers are shown.

    The full text of each paper in contention for the list is then screened: is the main theorem stated and proved in full, do the claims match the abstract, are there gaps or unresolved dependencies? Papers where the screen finds a real gap are left off the list; the others carry a short note. The morning list is first published from the abstracts and marked provisional until the full-text screen has run.

    Scores and screens are produced automatically by a language model. They are a reading aid, not peer review, and they will sometimes be wrong. Follow the links for the papers themselves.
