      How papers are selected

      Every paper on the day's arXiv math.CO listing (new submissions and cross-lists) is read from its abstract and given an internal score: an estimate of the level of journal the result belongs in, weighing the strength of the result against prior work, whether it settles a known problem, and how wide an audience will care — with extra weight on structural, extremal and probabilistic graph theory. The 30 highest-scoring papers make the list.

      The scores themselves are not published and the 30 papers appear in a random order (fixed for each day), so that the page reads as a shortlist rather than a ranking. Survey and expository papers are kept out of the shortlist and shown in their own section: at most three per day, chosen by how topical their subject is for this audience — on many days there are none.

      The selection is produced automatically by a language model from the abstracts alone. It is a reading aid, not peer review, and it will sometimes be wrong. Follow the links for the papers themselves.

      Using the page

      The menu at the top switches between listing days; the arrow keys do the same. The filter box narrows both lists to papers whose title, authors, arXiv identifier or abstract contain a word. "Compact abstracts" folds every abstract to three lines; click one to unfold it.

      The page is refreshed every weekday morning shortly after arXiv publishes the new listing. On days without an arXiv announcement (weekends and arXiv holidays) there is no entry.
